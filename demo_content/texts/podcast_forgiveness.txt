HOST: Welcome back to Grace Notes. I'm Maya Chen, and today we are talking about forgiveness with Pastor Owen Hartley. Owen, thanks for joining me.
GUEST: Thanks for having me, Maya. This topic is close to my heart.
HOST: Years ago, I had only been at that job one day when a coworker presented my idea as his own. It kept happening for months, and I carried that resentment for a long time. Why is forgiveness so hard?
GUEST: Because the hurt is real. Forgiveness is not pretending that nothing happened. It is choosing to release a debt that someone owes you, even when they have not paid it back.
HOST: Release a debt. That language shows up in prayer too, doesn't it?
GUEST: It does. Many of us say these words every week. Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors. Every time we pray that line, we tie the mercy we receive to the mercy we give.
HOST: But what if the same person keeps hurting you? Is there a limit?
GUEST: Peter asked that exact question, in Matthew eighteen, twenty-one and twenty-two. He wants to know how often he should forgive a brother who keeps sinning against him, and he suggests seven times. Jesus answers, not seven times, but seventy times seven.
HOST: So Jesus isn't handing us a bigger number to count toward.
GUEST: Exactly. He is telling us to stop keeping score. Forgiveness becomes a way of life instead of a transaction. That does not mean we ignore wisdom or safety. Forgiving someone and trusting them again are two different things.
HOST: Let's talk about the story most people picture when they hear the word grace.
GUEST: The prodigal son. A younger son takes his share of the family wealth, leaves home, and wastes all of it. He ends up feeding pigs, hungry and alone. Finally he heads home, practicing a speech about becoming one of his father's hired workers.
HOST: And the father never lets him finish that speech.
GUEST: Right. The father spots him while he is still far away, runs to him, hugs him, and throws a party. The son expected a lecture, and he got a celebration. That is what the forgiveness of God looks like.
HOST: But there's an older brother in that story too.
GUEST: Yes, and honestly, many church people are more like him. He stayed home, worked hard, and resented his father's mercy. The story ends with the father begging him to join the celebration, and we never find out what he decided.
HOST: So where does someone actually begin?
GUEST: Start with how much you have been forgiven. Paul says it simply in Ephesians four thirty-two. Be kind to one another, tender hearted, forgiving each other, just as God also in Christ forgave you. The key words are just as. We forgive out of the overflow of what we have already received.
HOST: Owen, any last word for someone who is carrying an old hurt today?
GUEST: Take one small step. Pray for that person by name, and ask God to help you release the debt. You may need to do it again tomorrow, and that is okay.
HOST: Thank you, Owen. And thanks for listening to Grace Notes.
