Good morning, church.
Today I want to talk about a question every one of us asks sooner or later.
Where is God when life falls apart?
Maybe you are asking that question right now.
Maybe you lost a job this year, or your marriage is struggling, or the doctor called with news you did not want to hear.
Suffering is real, and the Bible never asks us to pretend otherwise.
But the Bible does give us a hope that is stronger than our pain.
Please turn with me to Romans chapter eight verse twenty-eight.
Paul writes, “We know that all things work together for good for those who love God, for those who are called according to his purpose.”
Notice what this verse does not say.
It does not say that all things are good.
Cancer is not good.
Betrayal is not good.
Grief is not good.
What Paul says is that God works all things together for good.
Think of a weaver who takes dark threads and bright threads and makes one beautiful cloth.
That is what we mean when we talk about the providence of God.
God is not absent from your story, even in the chapters you would never have chosen.
And Paul does not stop there.
A few verses later, he asks whether anything at all could separate us from the love of Christ.
Could hardship do it?
Could hunger, or danger, or even death?
Paul goes on in verse thirty-seven: “No, in all these things we are more than conquerors through him who loved us.”
Not after all these things, and not around all these things, but in all these things.
Victory does not always mean escape.
Sometimes victory means that God holds you up while the hard thing is still happening.
Let me show you what this looks like.
Think about Joseph.
His own brothers were jealous of him, and they sold him as a slave.
He was carried off to a foreign land, falsely accused, and forgotten in a prison cell.
For years, nothing in his life looked good.
But God was at work in every hidden season.
In time, Joseph was raised up to help lead a nation, and he saved countless lives during a terrible famine.
When his brothers finally stood before him, afraid of what he might do, Joseph did not take revenge.
He told them that they had meant it for harm, but God had meant it for good, and because of it many lives were saved.
God was weaving the whole time.
My friend John called me last week.
He has been out of work for almost a year.
He said, “Pastor, I am tired of waiting on God.”
I did not have a quick answer for John.
But I reminded him of a promise God gave to his people when they were living far from home.
“For I know the thoughts that I think toward you,” says the LORD, “thoughts of peace, and not of evil, to give you hope and a future.”
Those words were first spoken to exiles who would wait many years to go home.
God did not promise them a quick fix.
He promised them his presence, and he promised them a future.
Now turn with me to James chapter one, verses two through four.
James says we should count it all joy when we fall into all kinds of trouble.
Why would anyone say that?
James gives us the reason.
The testing of our faith produces endurance.
And when endurance has finished its work, God makes us perfect and complete, lacking in nothing.
James is not asking us to enjoy pain.
He is inviting us to trust that God wastes nothing.
Your hardest season may be the very place where God is growing the strongest part of your faith.
So where is God when life falls apart?
He is right there, working all things together for good.
He was with Joseph in the prison.
He was with his people in exile.
And he is with you today.
Your story is not over.
Let us pray.
Father, thank you that nothing in our lives is outside your care.
For everyone here who is hurting, bring comfort and strength.
Where we cannot see the way forward, help us to trust your heart.
Fill us with hope, and teach us to live as more than conquerors through your love.
In Jesus' name, amen.
